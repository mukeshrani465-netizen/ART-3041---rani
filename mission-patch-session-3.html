SSEP Mission Patch · Session 3 · Homework

Using the tool to find new directions

I ran my Gravity Fading patch generator, pulled out two variations with potential, wrote down what works in each, and combined them into version 2 of the patch.

ART 3041 · A.I. for Designers · Fall 2026 · Assignment page: Session 3

1 · Two variations worth keeping

Both were made with my v1 tool (Mission Patch Generator).

01Coil orbit on a single line

A vertical line rises through the centre of a cream circle, with three violet dots fading from dark at the bottom to pale at the top. A continuous coil loops around the line like a spring and loosens as it climbs, so the object seems to unwind as gravity lets go. The double ring and curved BARUCH COLLEGE / ISS · 2027 rim text make it read as an official seal.

I chose it because it is the clearest version of "Gravity Fading": the darkness of the dots and the loose coil tell the whole story without extra imagery.

What I'd keep: the coil that opens with height, and the circular rim text.

02Subway map on night fabric

A transit-map route climbs with 45° bends across deep navy fabric, linking three ringed stations. The bottom station is bright white and each station above sinks into blue, so the route fades into space as it rises. The diagonal steps feel like a real subway map turned toward the sky, and the stitched texture, double border and small stars make it look like a real embroidered mission patch.

I chose it because it makes the Baruch part (the New York subway) obvious, and the dark background finally makes it read as space.

What I'd keep: the ringed station dots, the dark fabric with the white-to-blue fade, and the stitched finish.

2 · Patch version 2

One design that takes the strongest parts of both picks.

Digital mockup of v2, made from my notes.

A round patch on navy fabric. A subway route rises from the bottom with one 45° bend, and three ringed stations fade from white (Baruch) to blue (ISS). The coil from Variation 1 wraps only the upper part of the route, so the object starts floating once it leaves the ground.

  • From pick 1: the opening coil, the circle shape, curved rim text
  • From pick 2: the 45° subway bend, ringed stations, navy fabric, white-to-blue fade
  • New: the coil starts only after the launch station, so "lift-off" has a visible moment
  • Rim text: BARUCH COLLEGE (top) · ISS · 2027 (bottom), white thread

3 · Questions about the new design

From the Session 3 homework.

What message do you see?
Something leaves Baruch on an ordinary subway line and gets lighter until it floats in orbit. A Baruch experiment is going to space.
What feels distinctly Baruch?
The subway route and ringed stations. Baruch is a commuter school in Manhattan, and the subway is how most students get there.
What feels distinctly space research?
The fade into the dark fabric, which stands for loss of gravity, and the orbit coil. Together they show microgravity, the condition the experiment tests.
What image would you remember tomorrow?
A subway line climbing into a night sky and starting to spiral.
What would be lost if this symbol disappeared?
The link between everyday student life at Baruch and the space station. Without the route, the patch is just another orbit graphic.
What elements of your variants would you like to see in your new design?
The opening coil from Variation 1, and the bends, ringed stations, navy fabric, light-to-dark fade and stitched finish from Variation 2.
